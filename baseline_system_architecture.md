# Trading System Architecture: XGBoost + Genetic Algorithm

## Competition Constraints
- No day trading — never buy and sell the same symbol same-day (WInS rule)
- No short selling, no margin — long-only
- Position limit: a cap on % of portfolio in any single stock (value TBD — confirm in WInS Portfolio Summary → Rules once available, then hardcode in `config/cost_model.py`)
- Maximum 200 trades over a 10-week period — the actual binding constraint (see below)
- Maximum 2 trades per stock per day — effectively non-binding: hitting it on a given stock would require the same-day entry+exit that "no day trading" separately prohibits. Kept here because it's a real rule, not because the system is expected to approach it.
- All execution is manual via WInS — the system's only output is a ranked recommendation; a human places every order
- These constraints mean the system must optimize for **selectivity**, not raw predictive accuracy

---

## 1. System Layers

### Layer 1: Data
- Source: Yahoo Finance (`yfinance`), **daily bars**
- Universe: ~685 tickers that were ever S&P 500 members across 2018–2026, survivorship-bias-free via point-in-time membership (`data/processed/sp500_membership.csv`) rather than today's roster applied backward
- Store in a unified, columnar format for fast loading
- Cleaning and feature engineering (moving averages, volatility, sector tags) happens here, mostly CPU/disk-bound

**Bar frequency — decided 2026-08-16, daily not hourly:**
- Originally assumed hourly, from a rows-per-ticker estimate that was never checked against what Yahoo actually serves. Verified: `yfinance` caps intraday (1h) data at ~730 days *measured from today*, not per request — chunking cannot walk further back than that. Hourly bars covering the full 2018–2025 window are not obtainable without a paid vendor (Polygon, Databento, Alpaca, Tiingo); that's a deferred option, not a pursued one.
- Even if it were available, intraday resolution buys nothing here: **no day trading is allowed**, so a signal can't be acted on and exited within the same session. Hourly's only structural advantage — more decision points per day — is unusable by rule.
- Row count was the wrong tradeoff to optimize. Hourly-capped-at-730-days actually has *more total rows* than 8 years of daily (~9.7M vs. ~6M at ~3,000 tickers) — what it costs is **calendar coverage**, 2 years vs. 8. A model that needs to survive regime shifts needs the 8 years of distinct regimes daily bars provide, not the extra row density hourly would add from inside a single regime.
- Net: daily bars over the full ~8yr history is both the only feasible option and the correct one under this competition's rules.

### Layer 2: Sector Experts
- Universe split by sector (tech, financials, real estate, etc.)
- One XGBoost model trained per sector, on that sector's data only
- Each sector expert gets its own genetic algorithm search (population of hyperparameter candidates: tree depth, learning rate, number of trees, etc.), evolved against its own validation slice

### Layer 3: Broad Model
- One XGBoost model trained across the full universe, all tickers pooled
- Captures general, market-wide patterns (momentum, volatility clustering) that aren't sector-specific
- Gets its own separate genetic algorithm search

### Layer 4: Combination
- **Starting approach: weighted blending.** Broad model and relevant sector expert predictions are combined via a weighted average, weights tuned during the genetic algorithm search.
- **Future exploration: stacking.** Sector expert + broad model predictions become input features to a small final model that learns how much to trust each expert. More powerful but needs its own training data to learn on, which is expensive given the short competition window — revisit once more historical backtest seasons are banked.

### Layer 5: Trade Selection (built on top of layers 2-4)
- Not just "will this go up" but "how confident is the model" — via prediction intervals or agreement between broad model and sector expert
- **Long-only**: no short selling, no margin — only act on positive predicted-edge signals
- **Cost-clearing filter**: only flag a trade if predicted edge clears commission + estimated spread by a margin (`config/cost_model.py`) — an "opportunity" that doesn't survive costs isn't one
- **Position sizing**: a parametrized rule — equal-weight to start, confidence-weighted or vol-scaled are candidates — capped at the per-stock position limit. A calibrated rule tuned by backtest grid search, not a second trained model; trade counts are far too small to fit one without overfitting.
- **Reads current portfolio state** (`data/processed/portfolio_state.json`, updated by hand after every real trade — WInS has no API) before sizing new trades, since the system has no other way to know what's already held
- **Exit rule: fixed holding period** (`exit_rule_spec.md`). A position entered on day `d` closes on `d + hold_days` (N), unconditionally — no stop-loss, no take-profit, no signal-reversal exit. `hold_days` isn't a knob independent of the model: it *is* the training label (N-day forward return), so it's swept as a full retrain per value (§3), not tuned by the GA. The 200-trade budget is therefore ~100 round trips, not 200 opportunities — every entry reserves a unit for its own future exit.
- Trade budget tracker: counts down from 200 (as entry+exit pairs), paces spending across the 10 weeks rather than front-loading
- Expected value ranking: combines predicted return size with confidence to pick the top slice of opportunities, since only ~100 round trips total can be made across the whole universe and window
- No day trading, satisfied by construction: an entry and its exit always fall on different days (hold_days ≥ 1), and a ticker exiting on day `d` cannot be re-entered on that same `d`

## 2. Training & Validation Plan

### Training Data
- Train on the full ~8 years of history, not just a window matching the 10-week competition length — more history means the model learns real patterns instead of noise from one short period
- Weight recent months more heavily in the loss/sampling, so the model stays tuned to current market regimes rather than treating 2019 the same as this year

### Validation Approach
- Use walk-forward validation throughout: train only on past data, test only on future data, never let the model see ahead
- Standard accuracy-based validation is not sufficient given the trade cap. Add a **"top-200 selection quality" backtest**: instead of scoring every prediction the model makes, simulate the model only acting on its top-ranked ~200 opportunities across a historical window, and score performance on that slice specifically. A model can have mediocre average accuracy but strong top-slice accuracy — that's what actually matters under this constraint.

### Trade Definition Under the Competition's Rules
- No day trading means a trade is a same-symbol entry on one day and an exit on a later day — never both within one session
- On daily bars this is the natural unit of decision anyway: one ranked score per ticker per day, one action (or no action) per ticker per day
- The 2-trades/stock/day cap from the rules is real but isn't expected to bind under this design — hitting it would require exactly the same-day round trip that's separately prohibited

### Compute: CPU, not GPU — decided 2026-08-16
- The Arc B70 isn't usable here at all: XGBoost's GPU acceleration (`device="cuda"`) is NVIDIA-only — confirmed via `xgboost.build_info()` on the installed build (`USE_CUDA: True`, `USE_NCCL: True`, no SYCL/oneAPI backend). `theta-core`'s GPU usage (`torch.xpu`) works because PyTorch ships a native Intel GPU backend; XGBoost has no equivalent.
- Even setting hardware aside, GPU is the wrong lever for this workload's shape. The GA search's cost is thousands of small, independent fits (7 features, ≤~1.4M rows for the broad expert), not one large model to accelerate — that's what GPUs are good at, not what this is.
- Benchmarked on the dev machine (i5-12600K, 16 threads) at realistic scale (400k rows, 7 features): a single fit goes from 3.25s (1 thread) to 0.97s (16 threads) — XGBoost's own CPU histogram parallelism already captures most of the available speedup. A first-pass test of running GA candidates across a multiprocess worker pool instead showed no further gain on top of that (~1.02x) — inconclusive, not a demonstrated win, so not pursued further without a real need.
- If GA search throughput becomes an actual bottleneck at full production scale (population 50 × generations 20 × 4 experts × up to 4 folds × 3 models/candidate), the productive next steps are early stopping (cut wasted boosting rounds past convergence) and a properly-tuned worker pool — not GPU acquisition, which wouldn't work with this library regardless of tuning.
- The entry+exit pairing is now literal, not just conceptual: the fixed-hold exit rule (`exit_rule_spec.md`) schedules every entry's exit at entry time, `hold_days` trading days later

| Selection score | `OverallModel.rank(ranking_score=...)`: raw predicted return vs. `vol_adjusted` (divide by recent realized volatility before ranking) vs. `uncertainty_adjusted` (**implemented** — rank by `combined_score - uncertainty_penalty * |broad_return - sector_return|`, a lower confidence bound built from broad/sector disagreement). First real-data backtest found the single top-ranked pick each day is a reliable loser (mean −4.1%, 62 trades, 60% concentrated in one ticker mid-merger) driven by argmax-over-noisy-predictions selecting outsized, high-volatility names; `vol_adjusted` demotes that real but only partially (mean improves to −2.8%, still the worst-performing rank) — residual is plausibly selection bias in the argmax itself, which volatility can't see and only a second, independent estimate (disagreement) can. Not yet run against real data (that's step 5) — the pre-registered predictions in open questions 8 and 9 are what this is being checked against. **Design decision made deliberately during implementation**: a ticker with no sector expert has no disagreement to measure, and defaulting that to zero uncertainty would read "no second opinion" as "maximally confident" and promote uncovered tickers — backwards, and it would silently confound the materials in/out ablation axis (a materials ticker flips between "has an estimate" and "doesn't" across arms). Handled by filling an uncovered row's uncertainty with that date's maximum observed disagreement among covered rows — the worst empirically-observed case, not zero — same shape as `vol_adjusted`'s own missing-data handling (fill with the max, not zero). Falls back to the global max for a date with zero coverage at all, and to 0.0 (no penalty) only in the fully-degenerate case of zero coverage anywhere in the frame. Tested directly (`tests/test_combiner.py`): a ticker with the single largest raw score but no sector coverage does not win rank 1 once a real disagreement penalty is available to beat it. |
| Agreement bonus on vs. off | `OverallModel(agreement_bonus=...)`: 0 disables it. First real `has_sector=True` run (Information Technology checkpoint) found agreement and accuracy aren't the same thing — two experts can share a blind spot on a chaotic name (broad model and a sector model can both read PARA's momentum features as extreme) as easily as they can confirm a real signal, and the bonus *amplifies* whichever direction they agree on. That's the opposite operation from `uncertainty_adjusted` (which *demotes* broad/sector disagreement) reading the same broad-vs-sector gap — potentially complementary, potentially redundant, potentially fighting each other. Not assumed to belong in the final combiner; run both ways once `uncertainty_adjusted` exists to find out. (A prior version of this bonus was additive in raw-return units and was found to dominate real predictions — fixed to multiplicative, which keeps it proportional to the prediction it reinforces; see `OverallModel.combine`'s comment.) Decomposed the fix's effect on the IT checkpoint rather than trusting the aggregate (`src.evaluation.decompose_selection_change`, now the standard tool for any scoring-change comparison — see `data/processed/agreement_bonus_fix_decomposition.json`): rank-1 is untouched by the fix (−4.11% broad-only → −3.62% with IT covered — that's step 4's job, not the blend's). The fix reshuffles 66.7% of all entries (663/994 dropped, 663/994 added, only 331 common to both scorings), and the apparent aggregate improvement (+0.25% → +0.43% mean/trade) is *not* statistically distinguishable from noise on that differing subset — block-effective SE (non-overlapping `hold_days`-wide trading blocks, not raw trade count, as the independence unit) gives t≈0.19, and even the maximally generous raw-n-no-overlap-correction case only reaches t≈0.77. Real mechanical fix (verified: bonus share of `combined_score` dropped from median 76% to ~4.6%), but no aggregate performance claim survives at this sample size — only training-path-validation and rank-1-untouched are load-bearing return findings from this checkpoint. One thing *is* a measured fact, not an estimate, and survives the effective-n problem entirely because it's a count: the dropped set concentrates into 30/100 available trading blocks (22.1 trades/active block) vs. the added set's 48/100 (13.8 trades/active block) — the fix spread trading activity across 60% more distinct time periods, a real reduction in temporal concentration risk regardless of whether mean return moved. See open question 8 for the pre-registered prediction this episode's common-set pattern (double-selected trades realizing worse than either side's unique picks) motivates for step 5. |
| Per-ticker frequency cap (**superseded — redundant once `vol_adjusted` is the ranking score, see below; kept as a tested, available option, not the standing default**) | `TradingConfig.max_ticker_share_pct` (None by default, off): caps any one ticker's share of the window's total entries, enforced in `TradeSelector._eligible`. Deliberately ticker-agnostic — no hardcoded exclusion list — both because no corporate-actions data exists to build one from (only Yahoo daily bars) and because a fitted list would be exactly the kind of sample-specific rule this project has already refused three times (rank 3–5, the bucket boundaries, skip-rank-1). ## 4. Open Questions / Where More Testing Is Needed

This is the ambiguity that current reasoning hasn't resolved — flagging explicitly so it gets tested rather than assumed. (Three questions from the original list are removed as resolved: hourly-vs-intraday checking frequency and GA interaction between model quality and timing strategy — both were about the timing/trigger layer, which doesn't exist under daily bars + no day trading, see the Bar Frequency decision in Layer 1 and the cut ablation rows above — and GPU vs. CPU training, see the Compute decision in §2.)

1. **What should the selection threshold actually be built from?** Candidates include prediction intervals, ensemble agreement between broad and sector models, and volatility — but which combination best predicts a genuinely good trade opportunity, and how strict the cost-clearing margin should be, is unverified.

2. **Weighted blending vs. stacking performance gap** — untested. Need enough banked backtest history to know whether stacking's added complexity is actually worth it, or whether blending captures most of the value.

3. **How much should recent data be up-weighted?** The right decay rate/recency weighting for the 8 years of training data against current market regime is unknown and needs to be swept as its own parameter.

4. **Pacing of the 200-trade budget** — even spend vs. front-loaded vs. confidence-adaptive pacing needs a direct backtest comparison; there's no a priori reason to assume one is better without testing on real historical windows.

5. **Sector boundary definitions** — how sectors are split (tech, financials, real estate, etc.) will affect how much each sector expert actually specializes vs. overlaps with the broad model. Different sector groupings should be tested for whether they meaningfully change expert quality.

6. **Cost model and position limit are placeholders.** Commission rate, assumed spread, and the per-stock position-limit percentage aren't confirmed yet (see `config/cost_model.py`, WInS Portfolio Summary → Rules) — the selection layer runs on placeholder values until the real numbers are filled in.

7. **Optimal `hold_days` (N) is unknown** — the sweep in `exit_rule_spec.md` §2 is how to find it, but expect it to interact with sector (a single global N may be suboptimal, though per-sector N multiplies retrain cost and is out of scope for the baseline). Whether unspent budget at window close means the selector was too conservative or correctly selective also isn't determinable from a backtest alone — needs comparison against a forced-full-budget variant.

8. **Pre-registered prediction for the agreement-bonus ablation (step 5).** Decomposing the bonus scale-fix's IT checkpoint found the *common* set — trades both the buggy and fixed scoring picked — realized worse (mean −0.23%, n=331) than either scoring's unique picks (+0.48% / +0.77%, n=663 each), though not significantly so (t≈0.4–1.1 depending on plausible block count and dispersion — same effective-n caveat as everything else here). Mechanistic account: surviving both scorings means scoring high on two different extremes at once (high agreement-bonus under the old formula, high predicted-return-share under the new one) — a stronger, compounded version of the same argmax/winner's-curse selection bias documented in the "Selection score" row, not a separate effect. If that account is right, it makes a falsifiable, checkable prediction once `uncertainty_adjusted` exists: **`agreement_bonus=off` should outperform `agreement_bonus=on`, and the common-set penalty should shrink or vanish under `agreement_bonus=off`.** Written down now, before step 5 runs it, so the result gets checked against this rather than fitted to whatever comes out. It can fail.

   **Known confound in this prediction and in `uncertainty_adjusted` generally, registered before step 5 runs so a null doesn't get over-read.** `uncertainty_adjusted`'s disagreement estimate (`|broad_return - sector_return|`) comes from exactly two point estimates. You cannot robustly estimate a spread from n=2 — it's a proxy of convenience, not a real ensemble variance. That means a null step-5 result is genuinely ambiguous between two different conclusions that would produce identical output: (a) *disagreement isn't a usable confidence signal* (the design's central bet is false), or (b) *n=2 is too few points to estimate uncertainty from* (the design might be fine; the measurement is just too weak to detect it). Nothing in the current implementation can tell these apart. The real fix — a seed-ensemble of the broad model (train k models on different seeds, use their prediction spread as the uncertainty estimate, replacing or supplementing the two-model disagreement) — is not built, deliberately: nothing has cleared significance yet to justify the cost (retraining broad k times, redesigning `ExpertModel`/`combine()` to carry an ensemble spread instead of one prediction), matching this project's standing rule of spending compute only after something shows promise. If step 5 comes back null, *that* is when the ensemble is worth building — it's what would let a real negative result be told apart from a measurement-too-weak one, which right now it can't be. Until then: a null on `uncertainty_adjusted` should be read as "not distinguishable from disagreement being unmeasurable at n=2," not as "disagreement is falsified."

9. **Re-run under fixed pacing (see the "Even pacing" ablation row) — direction confirmed, magnitude improved, conclusion unchanged.** Original (pre-pacing-fix) numbers: full coverage rank-1 n=21, mean −3.97%, median −7.29%, 81% PARA. Re-run: n=113, mean −3.60%, median −2.71%, win 37.2% — still clearly the worst-performing rank across broad-only (−0.63%), IT-only (−1.04%), and full coverage (−3.60%), and full coverage's own overall mean return/trade is now the lowest of the three (broad +0.54% > IT-only +0.24% > full coverage +0.12% — monotonically *worse* as sector coverage grows). Decomposed full-coverage vs. broad-only properly (`decompose_selection_change`, not a headline mean): common n=131 (−0.96%), only-broad-only n=863/99 blocks (+0.77%), only-full-coverage n=863/99 blocks (+0.28%), block-effective t=−0.45 — real direction, not significant, but a third independent line of evidence (alongside materials' own t=−3.52 and the monotonic-decline pattern) that the uncurated 11-sector roster is net-dragging on the blend. Original conclusion holds: broad and every sector model trained on the same 15-feature set read PARA's merger-driven price action the same wrong way, independent of which model or how many, so the fix is more likely feature/eligibility-layer than ensemble-architecture, and `uncertainty_adjusted` (demotes *disagreement*) structurally can't reach a shared-blind-spot case like this — Step 5 can still validate it on its own pre-registered terms (item 8), just shouldn't be read as having fixed PARA if it does.

   One other finding from the same full-coverage run: **measured, not estimated** — trades unique to full coverage (vs. broad-only) concentrated into just 21/100 trading blocks (44.2 trades/active block) vs. broad-only's unique trades spanning 63/100 (14.7/block) — a real deterioration in temporal spread, the opposite direction from the agreement-bonus fix's improvement (item 8's episode moved 30→48 blocks).

10. **Rank-1 entries are a structurally biased subsample (confirmed) — but the stale-momentum hypothesis this motivated turned out to be another PARA finding, not a selector-design flaw (resolved, cheaply, before rebuilding sector coverage on top of it).** Classified every one of 497 test days by why that day's #1-ranked candidate was or wasn't entered (post pacing-fix, broad-only): entered 108 (21.7%), already held 304 (61.2%), same-day re-entry ban 82 (16.5%), ineligible on cost/direction 0 (0.0%), pacing-zero-that-day 0 (0.0%, frequency cap wasn't even active in this run). The day's top pick is *never* rejected for being a bad prediction or for pacing — every rejection (77.7% of days) is "already in the book." So "rank-1" entries do specifically capture days a persistently-dominant name's `hold_days`-old position had just freed up and it was *still* #1 — real, general, and confirmed.

    What that sampling mechanism implies for *performance*, though, does not generalize. Tested directly: computed `k` (consecutive #1-ranked days ending on the entry date) for every rank-1 entry and checked whether return degrades as `k` rises. Pooled across all tickers it does — Spearman(k, return) ρ=−0.20, p=0.037 — but excluding PARA it vanishes entirely: ρ=−0.09, p=0.49. Every single k≥13 entry (n=7, mean −13.0%, win 14.3% — the whole extreme tail) is PARA; PARA's own k distribution (mean 5.9, max 25) dwarfs everyone else's (mean 2.0, max 9), so it's the only ticker whose streaks ever run long enough to test the hypothesis at all. **Conclusion: the stale-momentum-reentry effect is PARA riding the already-held sampling mechanism harder than anyone else, not a property of the mechanism itself.** No TradeSelector redesign motivated by this; the frequency cap (already built) is the right-shaped fix, since it targets exactly this kind of one-name-dominates-a-mechanism pattern without hardcoding which name.

    What *is* still open, and isn't explained by either the sampling mechanism or PARA: even at k=1 (freshest possible pick, PARA excluded), rank-1 entries are still mildly negative (n=36, mean −0.02%, median −1.17%, win 36.1%) — consistent with, and not resolved by anything here, the original argmax-selection-bias theory this project started from. That residual is the piece to carry into the sector-coverage rebuild, not the stale-momentum story.

11. **Step 5's ablation is underpowered by construction — the decision rule for a null result has to be set before the run, not after.** Original design: 3 `ranking_score` arms (raw / vol_adjusted / uncertainty_adjusted) × 2 `agreement_bonus` (on/off) × 2 materials (in/out) = 12 arms, split-sample with embargo = 24 measurements. Bonferroni-corrected across 12 comparisons, claiming a winner needs roughly t≈3.0. The highest |t| observed *anywhere* in this project, across every diagnostic run so far, is ~1.7 (the frequency cap's period-champion reading). The near-certain outcome of the ablation as originally scoped is therefore that nothing clears — and if that isn't decided in advance, the natural failure mode is promoting whichever arm happened to have the largest (statistically meaningless) point estimate after the fact, exactly the kind of overclaim this project has repeatedly had to retract elsewhere.

    Two changes made in response, both before step 5 runs:

    - **Cut an axis.** Materials already has decisive, sign-stable evidence (item 9's entry) — deciding it as part of a 4-axis ablation would spend statistical budget re-litigating something already settled, at the direct cost of power on the axis the ablation actually exists to test. Materials is now excluded from `EXPERTS` directly (`src/config.py`), independent of step 5. That takes the ablation to 3 × 2 = 6 arms, 12 measurements — a real, if partial, power improvement; still nowhere near t≈3.0-proof, but the honest amount of power this dataset actually has to give.
    - **Pre-registered null response.** If no arm clears the (recomputed, 6-arm) Bonferroni-corrected threshold — the expected outcome — the default is the simplest configuration: `ranking_score="raw"`, `agreement_bonus=0` (off), full (materials-excluded) roster, on the grounds that added complexity has to earn its place, not merely fail to be ruled out. The ablation's report is a table of effect sizes with confidence intervals per arm, not a named winner; whether anything cleared the corrected threshold is stated explicitly, not implied by which number happens to be largest.

    **Result (`scripts/step5_ranking_ablation.py`, `data/processed/step5_ranking_ablation.json`): the predicted null.** 5 comparisons vs. the baseline (`raw`, bonus off) → corrected threshold |t|≈2.58 (recomputed from the actual comparison count, not assumed). Highest observed: |t|=0.77 (`vol_adjusted` and `uncertainty_adjusted`, both bonus settings, essentially tied). **Nothing clears — default to `raw`/`agreement_bonus=0` per the pre-registered rule.** Not read as `uncertainty_adjusted` being falsified (see the n=2 confound, item 8) — this ablation could not distinguish any arm from the baseline at this sample size, which is a different claim from "the arms don't differ."

    **The null is a methodological result, not a disappointment — the pre-registered protocol predicted exactly this outcome (highest |t| anywhere in this project ~1.7, corrected threshold ~2.6-2.9) and it held.** That's the protocol working, not failing.

    Worth recording precisely despite the null, since it's the actual pattern in the numbers, not a claim about significance: `raw` rank-1: n=108, mean −3.59% (bonus off) / −3.44% (bonus on) — consistent with item 9's full-coverage finding, as expected. `vol_adjusted` rank-1: n=197, mean +0.62% / +0.96%. `uncertainty_adjusted` rank-1: n=156 / n=152, mean +0.13% / +0.02%, and the single largest overall-mean point estimate of any arm (+0.63%).

    **PARA's rank-1 share by arm, a count rather than a mean — measurable precisely at this n, and it corrects the framing above rather than just confirming it:** raw 42.6% / 41.7% (bonus off/on) → **vol_adjusted 3.0% / 3.6%** → uncertainty_adjusted 15.4% / 15.8%. `vol_adjusted` does almost all of the real concentration-breaking work here; `uncertainty_adjusted` does a real but much smaller fraction of it. This is exactly what item 9's structural finding predicts, now confirmed numerically rather than argued mechanistically: PARA's failure mode is broad/sector *agreement* on a shared blind spot, not disagreement, so `uncertainty_adjusted` — which only penalizes measured disagreement — structurally can't reach PARA's dominant picks the way `vol_adjusted` (agreement-blind, just divides by realized volatility) does. **Correction to the framing in the previous paragraph: "directionally consistent with the design's central bet across two independent mechanisms" overstated `uncertainty_adjusted`'s own contribution — most of what looked like shared support was `vol_adjusted` carrying the effect.** `uncertainty_adjusted`'s real, measured effect (42.6%→15.4-15.8%) is smaller than it appeared next to `vol_adjusted`'s, and it remains genuinely unclear how much of even that reduction is disagreement doing real work vs. the n=2-confound noise floor (item 8) — that question is still open, not resolved by this count. If the ensemble upgrade is ever built, this PARA-share-by-arm comparison is the direct thing to re-run against it.

12. **Measurement-target pivot: backtest return is the wrong ruler for everything remaining. Switch to IC.** Step 5's 6 arms differed by 0.2–0.6 percentage points of mean return/trade — resolving power given ~100 independent 5-day blocks in a 497-day window is roughly ±0.7–1.0pp, so those differences were close to or below what backtest return can see at all, independent of whether they're real. Per-date IC, computed directly across ~500+ names a day, resolves roughly an order of magnitude finer — every paired IC comparison already run in this project (broad vs. cross-sectionally-normalized features, the per-sector significance table, the materials sign-stability check) has landed at SE≈0.004–0.005, vs. backtest return's SE≈0.4–0.5pp on comparable data. Every remaining architecture/feature/hyperparameter question in this project — the seed ensemble, the full GA search, any feature change — is answerable against IC and was not reliably answerable against return. Built as a standard tool rather than reimplemented a fifth time: `src.evaluation.paired_ic_comparison` (paired per-date IC difference, mean/SE/t/CI — see `src/evaluation/ic_comparison.py`), backed by a new `per_date_cross_sectional_ic` extracted from `cross_sectional_ic`'s own (already-fast, vectorized) computation so the two never drift apart.

    **The GA gate is restated accordingly.** The standing rule through steps 1–5 was "keep the GA small until something clears significance in the backtest." As written, that gate is unsatisfiable with 497 days of daily bars — the achievable |t| ceiling observed anywhere in this project so far is ~1.7, against a Bonferroni-corrected bar of ~2.6–3.0 depending on comparison count; closing that gap at fixed daily-bar frequency would need roughly 11x more independent (non-overlapping, `hold_days`-wide) blocks than the ~100 this window provides — i.e., a backtest window an order of magnitude longer, not a better idea. **New gate: run the full ~20-generation GA search only once a change moves out-of-sample IC by more than 2 SE, computed via `paired_ic_comparison` against the current baseline (broad's own established +0.0209, ~4.6 SE from zero — the only unambiguously real number this project has measured) — not "clears significance in the backtest."** This supersedes every earlier "nothing has cleared significance yet" note in this document, all of which meant the backtest-return version of the gate.

    **Roadmap, in order of expected value, all pending this pivot:**
    1. **Seed-ensemble uncertainty** (resolves the n=2 confound, item 8) — not worth building until IC-based evaluation exists to actually see whether it helps; now it does.
    2. **Feature work** — where real edge would come from, if there is any. Fifteen price-derived features on daily bars is a well-mined space, and broad's own IC (+0.0209, ~4.6 SE) is the only clearly-nonzero thing measured anywhere in this project; improving that number is more promising than any further architecture change, all of which (agreement bonus, vol/uncertainty-adjusted ranking, sector coverage) have moved return by amounts this project's own tools show are indistinguishable from noise at this sample size.
    3. **The full GA search — last, and only if 1 or 2 move IC.** Tuning hyperparameters against a signal that hasn't itself been improved is the least valuable compute available; the search should chase a real, IC-validated improvement, not the current feature/architecture baseline.

    **What this rules out:** further architecture ablations scored against backtest return. Steps 4 and 5 demonstrated that path is measurement-limited, not idea-limited — the tool, not the ideas, was the constraint.

13. **Standing default revised: `vol_adjusted`, not `raw` — decided on concentration grounds, not the null return-based ablation, which never had standing to speak to this.** The pre-registered null response in item 11 ("if nothing clears, default to `raw`/`agreement_bonus=0`") covered *return*, which item 12 established is unmeasurable at this sample size for differences this small. Concentration is a different question, measured decisively rather than nulled: PARA's rank-1 share by arm (item 12) is 42.6%/41.7% under `raw` (bonus off/on) vs. **3.0%/3.6% under `vol_adjusted`** — a count-based result at real n, not a noisy mean, and not subject to the same power problem. A rule calibrated for an unmeasurable question shouldn't discard evidence on a measurable one. Standing default: `ranking_score="vol_adjusted"`, `agreement_bonus=0` (unchanged — no evidence bonus=on helps, at return or via the PARA count), `max_ticker_share_pct=None` (see the frequency-cap ablation row — tested and found redundant once `vol_adjusted` handles concentration upstream). `BASELINE_ARM` in `scripts/step5_ranking_ablation.py` updated to match.

    **Seed-ensemble uncertainty (item 12's roadmap item 1), deprioritized.** Its purpose was resolving the n=2 confound obscuring whether `uncertainty_adjusted`'s weak measured effect was a real-but-unmeasurable signal or a genuine null. The PARA-by-arm count (item 12) substantially answers the practical version of that question already: `uncertainty_adjusted` moves PARA's share (42.6%→15.4-15.8%), but `vol_adjusted` — cheaper, already built, agreement-blind by construction — moves it further (→3.0-3.6%) through a completely different, better-understood mechanism (dividing by realized volatility, not estimating a spread from two points). With a working, decisively-better alternative already in hand, resolving the finer question of "how good is disagreement really, once measured properly" is worth less than it was before `vol_adjusted`'s effect size was known, and it remains the more expensive of the two roadmap items. Not cancelled — the n=2 confound is still real and still registered (item 8) — just no longer the next thing to build.

    **GA search: unchanged, still last, only if feature work (item 12's roadmap item 2) moves IC.**

14. **Feature work, iteration 1: `sector_momentum_60_skip_10` (Moskowitz & Grinblatt 1999, industry momentum) — tested, rejected, decisively.** First run of the tight loop item 12 prescribes: add a feature, `paired_ic_comparison` against the current baseline, keep only on a real improvement past 2 SE. A ticker's sector's own leave-one-out momentum (`RealDataProvider._add_sector_momentum`, computed but not promoted to `FEATURE_COLUMNS`) — same genome/seed as broad's established model, only the feature set differs. Result: out-of-sample IC **+0.0209 → +0.0095**, paired t=**−2.69** — a real, decisive move in the *wrong* direction (roughly halving the one clearly-nonzero measurement in this project), not a non-result. Discarded. Best guess at the mechanism, not confirmed: a sector-wide average is structurally closer to a regime/market-level signal than this project's other features, which are deliberately idiosyncratic and scale-free within one ticker's own history — likely exactly the kind of common, per-date co-movement the cross-sectional target demeaning was built to screen out. Caveat on the test itself, honestly carried rather than hidden: reused broad's existing genome rather than re-running the GA for the 16-feature set, per the "GA last, only if IC moves" rule — a properly re-tuned model might behave differently, untested, and not worth testing given how decisive this reading already is. Kept in the code (off, documented) rather than deleted, same posture as the `normalize_features` negative finding — the point of the tight loop is a fast, cheap "no" like this one, not a slow one.

    **Follow-up check: does `sector_momentum_60_skip_10` explain PARA's argmax concentration?** Cheap, run before ruling out the whole momentum family on concentration grounds. Result: **no** — if anything the opposite. With the candidate feature in, PARA's raw daily argmax share *dropped* (49.3% → 29.0%, distinct winners 46 → 67), even though overall IC got worse. Momentum-family features are not the concentration mechanism; this candidate diluted PARA's dominance while also diluting the model's actual signal. Rules out a hypothesis rather than confirming one — worth recording precisely rather than silently dropping since it changes how future momentum-family candidates should be read (not automatically suspect on concentration grounds, even though this specific one failed on IC grounds).

15. **Fourth diagnostic-tooling bug caught this project — same lesson each time: a diagnostic's own decision logic needs a deliberate failing-case test before its output is trusted.** `scripts/feature_candidate_sector_momentum.py`'s original decision logic flagged "KEEP" on any `|diff| > 2 SE`, without checking sign — so candidate 1's real, decisive *degradation* (t=−2.69) printed KEEP instead of DISCARD. (Prior three: the `vol_normalized_rank_check` NaN crash, the split-sample year-boundary leak, and the Step 5 Bonferroni threshold needing to be recomputed per the actual comparison count rather than reused from a different arm count.) Fixed by extracting the decision into `src.evaluation.keep_or_discard` — a small, standalone, pure function, tested directly (`tests/test_ic_comparison.py`) against synthetic cases including the exact failing shape (a real, significant move in the wrong direction) that the original bug missed. Every future feature-work script calls this shared function rather than reimplementing the decision inline — same reasoning as promoting `decompose_selection_change` and `paired_ic_comparison` out of one-off scripts earlier in this project.

16. **Feature horizon audit: most of the existing 20-day+ features come from literature validated at a monthly-or-longer prediction horizon, not this project's 5-day one.** Reviewed all 15 committed features against `hold_days=5`: `momentum_5`, `jump_ratio`, and `volume_shock` are near-term by construction and match; `px_to_ma_20`, `momentum_60_skip_10`, `pct_from_252d_high`, `volatility_20`, `semivol_skew`, `max_ret_20`, `min_ret_20` all trace to literature (Jegadeesh 1990; Jegadeesh & Titman 1993; George & Hwang 2004; Ang et al. 2006; Bali/Cakici/Whitelaw 2011) validated at 1–12 month prediction horizons, not 5 days — a real, systematic pattern, not one-off. `realized_skew_20`/`realized_kurt_20`/`ret_vol_corr_20` are plausibly mismatched too but shortening them trades horizon-match for pure estimation noise (skew/kurtosis need enough points to mean anything) — a real bias-variance question, not a free fix. `amihud_20`/`log_dollar_volume` are slow-moving characteristics where the window question mostly doesn't apply. `pct_from_252d_high`'s mismatch can't be fixed by shortening — a 52-week high is definitionally a year of data, so the open question there is whether the effect transfers to 5 days at all, not a windowing fix. Lowest-risk, most literature-clean candidate from this audit: `px_to_ma_20` → `px_to_ma_10` (tested next, item 17).

25. **Feature work, iteration 5 (final): market-relative features (`beta_60`, `idio_vol_60`, `market_corr_60`) — tested as a family, pre-registered as the LAST feature family in this tight loop regardless of outcome. Clean null; stopping feature-work iteration now.**

    **Correction to item 24's own stopping-rule note before building this**: item 24 suggested `MARKET_TARGET_COLUMN` (the cross-sectional mean already computed for target demeaning) could serve as the market series "with no new data fetch." That's wrong, and worth recording as wrong rather than quietly building it differently — `MARKET_TARGET_COLUMN` is the cross-sectional mean of the FORWARD `hold_days`-ahead return, a label, not information known as of the feature date. Using it as a feature ingredient would be a severe lookahead bug (a stock's own beta computed partly against its own future return). No new data fetch either way — this is a genuinely free feature family, just not built the way item 24 described it.

    **Implementation**: 60-day rolling window (`momentum_60_skip_10` precedent), `min_periods=30` (this project's standard half-window convention). `idio_vol_60` via the closed-form OLS variance identity — `idio_var = var(stock) − beta² × var(market)`, exact for single-regressor OLS since the residual is orthogonal to the regressor by construction — rather than an actual per-window regression loop, with a `.clip(lower=0)` guard against floating-point error pushing a near-zero idiosyncratic variance fractionally negative (which would otherwise `sqrt` to `NaN`).     **Deliberate failing-case test, and it actually failed once, for a real reason, before being trusted** (`tests/test_real_provider.py::test_beta_and_idio_vol_match_closed_form_for_a_deterministic_relationship`): two synthetic tickers with returns constructed as exact scalar multiples of a shared common factor (X = 2×cf, Y = 1×cf), giving hand-computable closed-form beta/idio-vol/correlation (beta_X=4/3, beta_Y=2/3, both idio_vol≈0, both corr=1.0).
